# OR-45: media read retries use a deterministic schedule and honour retry-after for every reason they retry

TypeScript: `packages/ai/src/route/media.ts:152-176` builds `READ_RETRY` from `Schedule.exponential("1 second")` capped at 30 s (`:161`), eight recursions (`:162`), then `Schedule.jittered` (`:164`), and raises a delay to the provider's `retryAfterMs` only when the reason is `RateLimit` or `ProviderInternal` (`:166-175`); the schedule is applied to the queued GET at `:197`. The delay reads the reason's own `retryAfterMs` field (`:169-170`), not the `AIError.retryAfterMs` getter, which also falls back to a nested `rateLimit.retryAfterMs` for `RateLimit` and reads `QuotaExceeded` and `ProviderInternal` (`packages/ai/src/schema/errors.ts:186-191`). `isRetryable` accepts `x-should-retry: true` ahead of the per-reason defaults (`:197-231`).

Rust: `read_with_retry` (`crates/ai/src/media/route.rs:504-525`) retries at most eight times (`:514`) with a `1_000 * 2^retries` millisecond base (`:517`), raised to `error.retry_after_ms()` (`:518`) and capped at 30 s by the same expression, so the delays are exactly 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 30, 30 and 30 seconds with no jitter. `retry_after_ms` reads `RateLimit` (top-level or a nested `rate_limit.retry_after_ms`), `QuotaExceeded` and `ProviderInternal` (`crates/ai/src/schema/errors.rs:272-281`), while `is_retryable` admits any error carrying `x-should-retry: true` (`:283-306`), so a `QuotaExceeded` with that header waits the provider's retry-after where the TypeScript schedule keeps its exponential delay, and a `RateLimit` carrying only the nested value also raises the Rust delay where the TypeScript schedule ignores it.

Reason: the port uses a fixed schedule so time-based assertions are deterministic, and it applies the provider hint whenever an error it retries carries one instead of limiting the hint to two reason tags. The retry test `read_retry_records_exponential_delays` (`crates/ai/tests/all/media.rs:140-195`) asserts the eight delays, the 5 s retry-after override on the first attempt, and the single attempt for a non-retryable `InvalidRequest`. No queued-media cassette records a failed read, so no retry runs during replay and neither a delay nor its jitter is compared.
